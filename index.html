  2. 02Two takeout boxes look identical. What do you know about their ingredients?

  3. 03Shoaff et al. (2019) studied 205 adolescents. What kind of study was it?

02 · The basics

Hormones coordinate growth.

  1. 01

    Hormones carry signals.

    Hormones are chemical messages. They help coordinate growth.
  2. 02

    Receptors respond.

    A signal only matters where something is ready to receive it. Receptors are the listeners.
  3. 03

    Endocrine-disrupting chemicals interfere with hormone activity.

    That's what the name means: something from outside the body getting in the way of a signal.

Exposure starts with contact.

Food

Contact through eating and drinking.

Worth asking: what touched this food before I did?

Remember

The route depends on the chemical and the product. A plastic object alone does not reveal your exposure.

05 · The teen brain

Why study the teen brain?

Adolescence includes ongoing brain development and hormonal changes. Both are happening at once, which is exactly why researchers pay attention to this stretch of life.

What researchers study:

  • Attention
  • Behavior
  • Reward processing

Read carefully

Human results differ across studies.

06 · Two words, two meanings

Reward and addiction are different.

Reward

Reward processing

Reward processing helps guide motivation and learning.

It's everyday brain work: noticing what's worth doing again.

Addiction

Addiction

Addiction involves persistent substance use despite harm.

It's a condition, not a lab score.

Don't mix them up

A change in an animal reward task does not establish addiction in people.

08 · Weighing it

What does the evidence support?

Each step up asks more of the research. Most headlines skip a few.

  1. 01

    Animal experiments

    Effects under study conditions.

    What happened to animals under the conditions tested. Not what happens in people.

  2. 02

    Human observational studies

    Exposure and outcomes measured together.

    An association: exposure and outcome showed up together. Not proof that one caused the other.

  3. 03

    Human causation

    Requires stronger evidence across study designs.

    That something causes an effect in people. Neither of our two studies sits on this step.

12 · Your turn · 30-second edit

Fix the headline.

Headlines often claim more than a study found. Below is a made-up but typical headline about the Shoaff et al. study. Rewrite it so it says only what the study actually showed.

Why this matters

This is the skill the whole lesson builds. Before you believe or share a health headline, check it against how the study was designed. If the words go further than the evidence, fix the words.

The headline

Plastic chemicals cause teen addiction.

What the study actually did

  • Human observational study of 205 adolescents
  • Urine samples measured exposure markers
  • Teachers rated behavior
  • Reported associations for selected phthalate markers
  • Did not test whether plastics cause addiction

Shoaff et al., 2019. See the Two studies section.

Check three things

  1. What was measured? "Plastic chemicals" in general, or something more specific?
  2. Cause or link? Can an observational study show that one thing causes another?
  3. What was the outcome? Did anyone measure addiction?
